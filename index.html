        Guide

        How to use the DBA Study Companion

        Everything you need, without anyone to ask

        Read this once, about ten minutes. Come back to Problems and fixes when something does not behave the way you expect.

        Start here

        1. Open the link in Chrome, Edge or Safari and keep using the same browser on that device. Your work is saved in that browser. Nothing is sent to us; it only leaves your device if you turn on sync to your own Google Drive.
        2. Install it as an app (optional, recommended). On a computer, use the install icon at the right of the address bar. On iPhone or iPad, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open the browser menu and tap Install app or Add to Home screen. It then opens like an app and works offline.
        3. Add your course materials. Go to Materials, make a folder for each course, and drop in your PDFs, Word files and slide decks.
        4. Put your timetable in. Go to Calendar and import the Excel or CSV timetable your programme office sends. Then press Everything under "Add to your phone's calendar" to get reminders on your phone.
        5. Back up every week or two from Materials → Save a backup, and keep the file in OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud.

        The five screens

        Course
        Your home screen: what is coming up in the next 30 days, overdue items, the reading you were last on, and your level in the speed course.
        Read
        Where you read. A switch at the top chooses Study (read at your own pace, highlight, write, take notes) or Train (paced speed-reading with a comprehension check).
        Materials
        Your library: folders, search, file-type filter, and each material's status and due date.
        Calendar
        Your timetable, submission deadlines, exams and reminders, in a month view with a "Coming up" list.
        Progress
        Results of your placement tests and training sessions, with a chart of your effective reading rate over time.

        Adding materials

        • What you can add: PDF, Word (.docx), PowerPoint (.pptx), and plain text. Old .doc and .ppt files must be re-saved as .docx or .pptx first.
        • Where it goes: into the folder you have open. Open the folder first, then drop files.
        • A whole course folder at once: on a computer, use Choose folder… and point it at your OneDrive, Google Drive or desktop folder for that course. Run it again later and only new files are added. (Not available on iPhone or iPad; select several files instead.)
        • Clean-up: before adding PDFs and Word files, tick what to leave out: running headers and footers, page numbers, footnotes, reference lists, tables. The library then shows what was removed, so you can tell if it went too far. To change it for a file already added, remove the file and add it again.
        • Details: press Details on any material to rename it, move it to another folder, set a status (To read, In progress, Done) or a due date. Due dates appear in your calendar.

        Study mode

        • Moving around: use Previous and Next, the arrow keys, or type a page number. Your place is saved automatically.
        • PDFs have two views: Show printed page shows the page exactly as printed (best for tables, figures and two-column articles). Show as text shows clean, reflowed text (best on a phone).
        • Scroll view or Page view: printed pages scroll continuously inside the window by default. Switch to Page view if you prefer one page at a time, or when writing with your finger.
        • Text size: A− and A+.
        • My notes: a notepad for each material, saved as you type.
        • Highlights list: every highlight with its page, filterable by colour. Tap one to jump to it. Copy all highlights copies them with page references, ready to paste into your essay notes.

        Study session (the best way to learn a reading)

        1. Start: in Study mode, go to where you want to begin, choose 10, 15 or 20 minutes in Study session, and press Start study session.
        2. Preview: skim the headings and opening lines shown, and note what you want to find out.
        3. Listen and read along: the voice reads while you follow the highlight and mark key points. It stops by itself at the end of the section.
        4. Recall without looking: the text is hidden while you write the main argument, one piece of evidence and one open question. This is saved to My notes.
        5. Check: answer the comprehension questions. Leave review reminders ticked to get three reminders in your calendar (tomorrow, 3 days, a week).
        6. Review: when a reminder comes up, open the material, press ▶ Listen to highlights to hear only what you marked, and recall the argument again.

        Why this works: previewing gives your mind a structure, recalling from memory and spacing reviews over days are the two habits with the strongest evidence for lasting learning.

        Key points

        • In Study mode, press ✦ Find key points in the panel on the right. You get the main points of the reading, the key terms, and a Show in text link that takes you to each point on the page.
        • ▶ Listen to them reads the points aloud; Add to my notes puts them in My notes; Copy copies them.
        • Two ways it works: if your browser has built-in AI on this device (recent Chrome on a capable computer; Microsoft Edge is adding it), you get a written summary and key points. Otherwise the app picks the most important sentences from the reading itself, word for word. Either way nothing leaves your device and it costs nothing.
        • Best on proper text PDFs and Word files. On scans with damaged text, the points are only as good as the text.

        Listen (read aloud)

        • In Study mode press ▶ Listen. The reading is spoken sentence by sentence, with the current word highlighted and the page turning by itself. Press ❚❚ Pause to stop and ▶ Resume to carry on from the same word.
        • Start anywhere: double-tap (or double-click) any word and reading starts there. While it is reading, a single click on a word jumps there. You can also select words and choose Listen from here.
        • Read only a part: select the passage, then choose Listen to selection only. It stops at the end of your selection; the bar shows "Reading your selection only" until then.
        • Skip page numbers, headers and footers (on by default): running heads, page numbers, journal footers and "Downloaded from…" lines stamped on every page are not read aloud. Untick it to hear everything.
        • One or two columns: each page is laid out on its own, so title blocks, side panels, abstracts and one- or two-column text are read in the order you would read them. Words broken across two lines (like "perspec- / tive") are read and highlighted as one word.
        • Voice: the most natural voice on your device is chosen automatically and marked ★; robotic voices are hidden (tick All voices to see them). Speed runs from 0.8× to 2×. Pauses sets how long it breathes at full stops and paragraph ends.
        • Best voices by device: Windows and Surface: open the app in Microsoft Edge for Microsoft's Natural voices (they need an internet connection). iPhone and iPad: download a Premium or Enhanced voice in Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices. Android: Chrome's Google voices; more can be added in Settings → Text-to-speech.
        • You can keep highlighting and taking notes while it reads.

        Pens and highlighters

        The pen tray sits above the page. Pick a tool, use it, and press Select when you want to read without marking.

        Highlighters
        Yellow, green, blue, pink. Press on the first word and drag to the last; lift to save. Works in clean text and on the printed page.
        Eraser
        Drag or tap over highlighted words. Only the words you cross are removed.
        ✎ Note
        Drag over a passage and type a note. The passage shows a small pen mark; tap it later to read or change the note.
        Ink pens
        Blue, black, green and red ballpoints for writing and drawing on PDF pages. Adjust Thickness before writing.
        Ink eraser
        Drag across pen strokes to remove them.
        Undo
        Removes your last pen stroke.
        Clear marks here
        Removes all highlights and ink on the current page, after asking you to confirm.

        With a stylus (Apple Pencil, Surface Pen, Samsung S Pen): once the app sees your stylus, only the stylus marks and your finger scrolls, so your palm will not leave marks. Without a stylus, your finger marks while a pen is selected; scroll by dragging the grey margin, or press Select.

        Ink works on PDF pages only. Word files and slides reflow, so use highlighters and notes there.

        Scanned documents

        • A PDF made of photographed or scanned pages is added as a Scan. You can read it, highlight areas by dragging boxes, write on it with the ink pens and attach notes.
        • Turn a scan into text: open it in Study mode and press Turn into text under the page. Choose the pages and press Turn into text; the app reads each page on your device (a few seconds a page, free, offline after the first use). Then press Create a text material from the transcription to get a version you can listen to, train on and search.
        • Scanned journals with damaged text (common on older JSTOR copies): when you add one, lines whose hidden text is cut short are read again from the page image automatically, so the missing words come back. This happens once, while the file is added.

        Speed and comprehension course

        Read this first. Reading much faster than your natural pace costs understanding. The course therefore scores your effective rate: your speed multiplied by your comprehension. It only moves you up when your understanding holds.

        1. Placement test. Read a short business passage at your normal pace and answer five questions. This sets your baseline and the target speed of all ten levels.
        2. Train. Press Start level on the Course screen, open any reading, and press play. Read for 10 to 15 minutes.
        3. Choose where to start. Click any word on the page (or on the printed PDF page), type a page number, or in Study mode select text and choose Train from here. Use this to skip contents pages, tables and front matter.
        4. Check. Press Finish session and check comprehension and fill the gaps from the passage you just read. Gap-fill tests recall of wording, so add your own test: before looking back, write the author's main argument in two sentences.
        5. Report card. A level passes at 80% comprehension on at least 300 words at the target speed.
        6. Retest every two weeks on the Course screen. A fresh passage you have never seen is the true measure of progress.

        Ladder drill. In Train mode, choose Set up ladder drill. You read the same passage for 5, then 4, 3 and 2 minutes (or 3, 2, 1). Round 1 is at your comfortable pace; each later round sets the speed so the same words fit the shorter time. Then you read 3 minutes of new text at 15% above your round 1 pace and take the comprehension check. Rereading gets easier partly because the words are familiar, so judge your progress by the new-text step and its score. Results are saved under Progress → Ladder drills.

        Controls in Train mode: speed (100 to 1,000 words per minute), words per chunk (1 to 10), display (clean text, printed page, focus point), pauses at punctuation, warm-up and sprint drills. Keys: Space play/pause, ← → previous/next sentence, ↑ ↓ speed, [ ] chunk size.

        Calendar and deadlines

        • Your programme timetable: import the file your programme office sends. If they send a new version, import it again; the old sessions from that file are replaced.
        • Import any timetable: Excel or CSV. It understands a grid timetable (dates down the side, time slots across the top) or a list with Date, Time and Subject columns. You see a preview before anything is added. Importing an updated version of the same file replaces the old one and keeps your own deadlines.
        • Course names: type the full name next to each code. Names then show everywhere.
        • Add a deadline or event: title, type (class, submission deadline, exam, reminder), date, optional times, where or how to submit, and notes. Deadlines and exams show a countdown.
        • Reminders on your phone: press Everything or Deadlines and exams only. Open the downloaded file: iPhone and Mac offer to add all events; Outlook imports it directly; for Google Calendar, go to calendar.google.com → Settings → Import. Reminders come 3 days and 1 day before deadlines and exams, and 30 minutes before classes. After adding new deadlines, export again (deadlines only is quickest).

        Your data, privacy and backups

        • Nothing is sent to us. There are no accounts, no analytics and no server behind this app. Your materials, highlights, ink, notes, calendar and results stay in this browser on this device. Nobody else can see them, including whoever shared the link.
        • Sync across devices (optional): press ☁ Sign in to sync at the top and sign in with Google. Your work is kept in a private app folder in your own Google Drive and stays the same on your laptop, phone and tablet. It syncs by itself about a minute after changes while you are signed in; after an hour Google asks you to tap ☁ Tap to sync again. Without sync, each device keeps its own library; use a backup to move your work.
        • Backups: Materials → Save a backup downloads one file with everything except the original files (tick Include original files to keep those too; the file is then much larger). Restore from backup replaces what is in the app with the backup.
        • What can erase your data: clearing your browser's history and site data, uninstalling the browser, using a private window, and on iPhone or iPad, not opening the app for a few weeks. A recent backup protects you from all of them.
        • Keep your originals in OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud. Treat this app as your reading desk, not your only copy.

        Problems and fixes

        My materials or highlights have disappeared

        Check you are on the same device, in the same browser, at the same web address, and not in a private window. If the browser's data was cleared, use Restore from backup.

        How do I get the latest version?

        Close and reopen the app while connected to the internet. Updates load automatically; your data is not affected.

        "No readable text found" when adding a file

        For a PDF this should now be added as a Scan. If not, the file may be protected or damaged; open it and save a fresh copy as PDF. For .doc or .ppt, re-save as .docx or .pptx.

        The highlighter does nothing

        Make sure a highlighter colour (not Select or an ink pen) is pressed in the pen tray. Press on a word, not on the white space, and drag. With a stylus, your finger only scrolls; use the stylus to mark.

        I cannot scroll while a pen is selected

        Without a stylus your finger draws. Drag the grey margin to scroll, press Select, or switch to Page view.

        The clean text has odd breaks or leftover headers

        Use Show printed page for that document, or remove it and add it again with different clean-up options. Two-column journals are the hardest case.

        A page is blank

        Scroll slightly or wait a moment; pages are drawn as they come into view. If it stays blank on an iPad, reduce the text size with A− and try again.

        "Attach PDF" appears instead of the page

        The original file is not stored here, for example after restoring a backup made without original files. Attach the same PDF once.

        "The file reader could not load"

        The PDF, Word, slides and spreadsheet readers download the first time you use each one. Connect to the internet once and try again; after that they work offline.

        The timetable import found nothing

        The file needs dates in the first column (grid layout) or a header row with Date and Subject columns. Check that the dates are real dates or written like 27/10/26.

        How the course works

        1. The placement test measures your normal reading speed and comprehension on a standard passage. That is your baseline.
        2. Each of the 10 levels sets a target speed relative to your baseline, plus a chunk size and drill.
        3. Read any material at the level's settings for 10 to 15 minutes, then take the check.
        4. A level passes at 80% comprehension or better at the target speed.
        5. Retest every two weeks on a fresh passage to see real progress on unseen text.
